Clarivate suppresses journals from the Journal Citation Reports when self-citations or concentrated citations from
another journal (``citation stacking'') distort their Journal Impact Factor. Its data are closed, so independent
checks need open data. We built yearly journal citation networks from the OpenCitations snapshot of January 2024
(1.96 billion citations; 85,437 journals) and compared them with Clarivate's suppression lists for data years
2015--2023, collected from primary sources. Where reference lists are present, open data reproduce Clarivate's
published self-citation shares within ten percentage points for 36 of 50 journals. The failures follow publishers
that do not deposit references with Crossref, which also hides several stacking donors. Biodiversity data downloads
registered with DataCite further inflate naive impact-factor numerators of taxonomy journals. The leading open method
for stacking groups, CIDRE, detects none of 15 named Clarivate pairs. It flags the stacking citations but drops large
donor journals, and lowering its donor threshold merges the pairs into large groups instead. A recipient-centric
reading of CIDRE's own test flags stacking-listed journals 6 to 14 times more often than other journals,
depending on consensus level, and 5 to 7 times more often than size-matched controls, sometimes years before the
listing. At the same flag rate it finds more listed journals than a plain donor-share rule. A prospective check on
the 2024--2025 lists agrees in direction but is not significant (one of six). Flagged pairs show more
citations per citing paper and weaker topical similarity than matched pairs. Open audits are feasible if coverage gaps
are reported.
